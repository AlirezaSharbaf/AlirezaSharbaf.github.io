A digital twin for surgical scheduling

A surgical schedule assigns cases to rooms before the day begins. With colleagues at MD Anderson, I studied what happens to that plan when an operation takes longer than expected or a case is canceled.

What can one late case change?

Imagine two operating rooms and three cases. Case B is scheduled after A in Room 1. Room 2 becomes available at 9:30. This fictional morning shows how a delay in one room can create a scheduling decision in another.

What the SPIE study compared

We paired schedule optimization with a digital twin, a simulation of how a surgical day unfolds across operating rooms. We used it to ask how a plan responds when a case takes a different amount of time than expected or when a case is canceled.

Replaying the same disruption under different scheduling choices lets us examine a practical trade-off. A change can make a room available for another case while shifting when the day ends elsewhere in the suite.
